# Artist Management Blueprint

Distilled from the art manager conversation export, 2026-09-10.

## The engagement

Andrea is acting as art manager for a client artist. The manager handles the business and career
side so the artist can keep making work: strategy, sales, exhibitions, marketing, money, relationships
and admin.

## The client, in one paragraph

Fine arts degree specialising in typography, from a well-regarded art college. No public recognition
yet. Wants six things: fashion collaborations, an artist magazine, residencies, exhibitions,
recognition, and process reels. The visual references he admires point somewhere quite different from
typography: containment and vitrines, hands breaking through the picture plane, hyperrealism used for
unease, scale distortion, restrained palettes with one loud accent, and the senses as subject matter.

## The blocking question

**Does the reference style match what he actually makes today, or is it aspirational?**

This was raised in the source conversation and never answered. Almost everything downstream depends
on it:

- The residency shortlist changes completely. Sculptural and material practice points to Japan and
  Italy. Experimental typography points to design-led and media residencies.
- The fashion pitch changes. Typography specialisation is a concrete, sellable hook for apparel
  brands. Surrealist containment work is a slower, more conceptual sell.
- The signature series changes. You cannot brief 3 to 5 signature pieces without knowing whether he
  is currently making objects or letterforms.

Resolve this before spending effort on applications. It is a single conversation with the artist.

## Sequencing

The tailored plan from the export, in order:

1. **Define the signature intersection.** Type plus surrealism plus containment, expressed as 3 to 5
   original pieces. This is the foundation everything else cites.
2. **Film process reels** around those specific pieces, not generic painting content.
3. **Build the artist magazine** as a visual artifact in his own aesthetic, editorial and campaign
   style. It works as a credibility tool before any institution has validated him.
4. **Target fashion brands** whose past campaigns already share the visual language, conceptual and
   surreal rather than plain product collaborations.
5. **Apply to residencies matched to his materials**, glass, plaster, mixed media, experimental
   typography, rather than generalist open calls.
6. **Pitch themed group exhibitions** on surrealism, identity, perception and materiality, rather
   than generalist open calls.
7. **Package the press story** once 2 or 3 proof points exist.

The logic is that residencies and brand collaborations have long lead times and do not require prior
fame, so they run in parallel with the studio work, and recognition compounds from stacked, documented
proof points.

## Intake checklist

Not yet collected from the client. Needed before pitching anyone.

**Portfolio inventory:** high-quality images of all available work · title, medium, dimensions, year
and edition per piece · which pieces are for sale.

**Pricing:** current or desired price per piece or a formula · negotiability range · past sales.

**Background:** bio covering education, exhibitions and awards · artist statement · existing press,
social media and website.

**Goals and boundaries:** the primary goal, whether that is sales income, exposure, following or
gallery representation · preferred venue types · how much involvement and approval he wants.

**Logistics:** framing and mounting status · who is responsible for transport · insurance.

**Commercial terms:** expected commission split for the manager · consignment versus guaranteed fee.

## Manager skills to keep sharp

Negotiation, networking, marketing and branding basics, organisation, basic financial literacy,
communication, an eye for market fit, and contract literacy.
